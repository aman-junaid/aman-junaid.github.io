## 1. Start with the accounts you actually use

Think about where your money lives: a bank account, a wallet, or another everyday account. Use **Accounts** to review your accounts before recording transactions. Start with the few you use most often.

## 2. Record one expense

Open the transaction editor from Home’s add action. Choose **Expense**, enter the amount and date, then select the account and category. Add a short description that will make sense when you come back to it, then save.

For example, a lunch purchase might use your everyday bank account and a food category. Use your own amount and date.

## 3. Keep income and transfers distinct

Choose **Income** when money comes in, such as a salary. Choose **Transfer** when you move money between your own accounts, and select both the source and destination.

> Moving money from your bank account to your cash wallet is a transfer. Recording it as an expense would make spending harder to interpret.

## 4. Look back at the end of the day

Home shows a monthly overview and recent transactions. Open **View all** for the full transaction list. Check the date, account, category, and amount of the entries you added. Open a transaction to correct it if needed.

## 5. Build a small routine

- Record purchases while they are still easy to remember.
- Reuse categories consistently so monthly comparisons stay useful.
- Review **Insights** to explore spending breakdowns and trends.
- [Set up a backup]({{ '/guides/backup-and-restore/' | relative_url }}) before you rely on a growing history.

Once the basics feel comfortable, [organize with labels]({{ '/guides/accounts-and-labels/' | relative_url }}) or [try Google Sheets]({{ '/guides/google-sheets/' | relative_url }}).
